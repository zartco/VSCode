# U1.4 Simplifying Expressions with Exponents

## Learning Objectives
- Perform math operations on polynomials by successfully simplifying and rewriting expressions containing exponents.

## Laws of Exponents

In order to perform math operations on polynomials, you need to know how to perform operations on terms that have exponents. Consider the Laws of Exponents presented in the following table. These laws are extremely helpful for simplifying or rewriting expressions.

| Law | Explanation | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **$x^1 = x$** | Any base raised to the exponent $1$ equals the base. | $5^1 = 5$ |
| **$x^0 = 1$** | Any non-zero base raised to the exponent $0$ equals $1$. | $4^0 = 1$ |
| **$x^{-1} = \frac{1}{x}$** | Any base raised to the exponent $-1$ equals the reciprocal of the base. | $7^{-1} = \frac{1}{7}$ |
| **$x^m x^n = x^{m+n}$** | To multiply exponential expressions with the same base, keep the base and add the exponents. | $x^3 \cdot x^2 = x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x$<br>$x^3 \cdot x^2 = x^5$ |
| **$\frac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}$** | To divide exponential expressions with the same base, keep the base and subtract the exponent of the denominator from the exponent of the numerator. | $\frac{x^5}{x^3} = \frac{x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x \cdot x}{x \cdot x \cdot x} = x \cdot x$<br>$\frac{x^5}{x^3} = x^2$ |
| **$(x^m)^n = x^{mn}$** | To raise an exponential expression to an exponent, keep the base of the exponential expression and multiply the exponents. | $(x^3)^2 = x^{(3 \cdot 2)}$<br>$(x^3)^2 = x^6$ |
| **$(xy)^n = x^n y^n$** | Any product raised to an exponent equals the product of the individual factors raised to the same exponent. | $(xy)^2 = x^2 \cdot y^2$ |
| **$\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^n = \frac{x^n}{y^n}$** | Any quotient raised to an exponent equals the quotient of the individual values raised to the same exponent. | $\left(\frac{x}{y}\right)^3 = \frac{x^3}{y^3}$ |
| **$x^{-n} = \frac{1}{x^n}$** | Any base raised to a negative exponent equals the reciprocal of the base raised to the opposite exponent. | $x^{-5} = \frac{1}{x^5}$ |

---

## Eliminating Negative Exponents

Sometimes, a problem may ask you to express an exponential expression without any negative exponents. You can apply the laws of exponents to rewrite any expression using only positive exponents. 

> [!IMPORTANT] 
> An expression is **not** considered fully simplified if it contains any negative exponents.

Practically speaking, a base raised to a negative exponent is in the wrong part of the fraction. In other words, a negative exponent in the denominator indicates the base belongs in the numerator and vice versa. Once you have moved the factors to where they belong, the negative sign on the exponent is no longer needed.

---

## Examples and Practice

### Example 1: Basic Simplification
**Simplify the expression:** $5^{-2}$

**Solution:**
$$5^{-2} = \frac{1}{5^2} = \frac{1}{25}$$

### Example 2: Multiplying Expressions with Exponents
**Simplify the expression:** $(2x^2y^3x^2y)^5$

**Solution:**
First, combine the terms inside the parentheses:
$$(2x^2 y^3 x^2 y)^5 = (2x^4 y^4)^5$$
Next, apply the exponent of $5$ to each individual factor:
$$(2x^4 y^4)^5 = 2^5 x^{20} y^{20} = 32x^{20}y^{20}$$

### Example 3: Dividing Expressions with Exponents
**Simplify the expression:** $\frac{x^6}{x^3}$

**Solution:**
$$\frac{x^6}{x^3} = x^{6-3} = x^3$$

### Example 4: Dealing with Complex Negative Exponents
**Rewrite the expression without negative exponents:** $\frac{12x^3 y^2}{3x^{-1}y^{-4}}$

**Solution:**
To combine the numerator with the denominator, apply the law $\frac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}$, and divide the coefficient of the numerator by the coefficient of the denominator.
$$\frac{12x^3 y^2}{3x^{-1}y^{-4}} = 4x^{3 - (-1)} y^{2 - (-4)} = 4x^{3+1} y^{2+4} = 4x^4 y^6$$
This step successfully removed all negative exponents.

### Example 5: Remaining Negative Exponents
**Rewrite the expression without negative exponents:** $\frac{2x^4 y^{-2}}{x^{-1}y^2}$

**Solution:**
First, apply the division law $\frac{x^m}{x^n} = x^{m-n}$:
$$\frac{2x^4 y^{-2}}{x^{-1} y^2} = 2x^{4 - (-1)} y^{-2-2} = 2x^{4+1} y^{-2-2} = 2x^5 y^{-4}$$
This step removed some negative exponents, but one still remains. 
Next, apply the negative exponent law $x^{-n} = \frac{1}{x^n}$. The reciprocal of $y^{-4}$ is $\frac{1}{y^4}$.
$$2x^5 y^{-4} = \frac{2x^5}{y^4}$$

### Example 6: Exponents Raised to Exponents
**Rewrite the expression without negative exponents:** $\left(\frac{x^{-3}}{x^{-6}}\right)^{-1}$

**Solution:**
Combine the numerator with the denominator:
$$\left(\frac{x^{-3}}{x^{-6}}\right)^{-1} = \left(x^{-3 - (-6)}\right)^{-1} = \left(x^{-3+6}\right)^{-1} = \left(x^3\right)^{-1}$$
Apply the power of a power law $(x^m)^n = x^{mn}$:
$$\left(x^3\right)^{-1} = x^{3(-1)} = x^{-3}$$
Finally, take the reciprocal to eliminate the negative exponent:
$$x^{-3} = \frac{1}{x^3}$$

### Example 7: Distributing Negative Exponents
**Simplify the expression to eliminate negative exponents:** $\left(\frac{x^{-3}}{5^{-1}}\right)^{-2}$

**Solution:**
Distribute the outer exponent to the numerator and denominator:
$$\left(\frac{x^{-3}}{5^{-1}}\right)^{-2} = \frac{(x^{-3})^{-2}}{(5^{-1})^{-2}}$$
Multiply the exponents:
$$\frac{x^{(-3)(-2)}}{5^{(-1)(-2)}} = \frac{x^6}{5^2} = \frac{x^6}{25}$$

### Example 8: Simplifying with Constants and Variables
**Simplify the expression to eliminate negative exponents:** $\frac{4^{-1} x^{-2} y^3}{x^3 y^{-2}}$

**Solution:**
Combine bases and move the constant to the denominator:
$$\frac{4^{-1} x^{-2} y^3}{x^3 y^{-2}} = \frac{x^{-2-3} y^{3 - (-2)}}{4^1} = \frac{x^{-5} y^5}{4}$$
Move the term with the negative exponent to the denominator:
$$\frac{x^{-5}y^5}{4} = \frac{y^5}{4x^5}$$

### Example 9: Negative Exponent with a Negative Result
**Simplify the expression to eliminate negative exponents:** $\left(\frac{x^{-5}}{x^{-4}}\right)^{-2}$

**Solution:**
Combine terms inside the parenthesis:
$$\left(\frac{x^{-5}}{x^{-4}}\right)^{-2} = \left(x^{-5 - (-4)}\right)^{-2} = \left(x^{-5+4}\right)^{-2} = \left(x^{-1}\right)^{-2}$$
Multiply the exponents:
$$x^{(-1)(-2)} = x^2$$

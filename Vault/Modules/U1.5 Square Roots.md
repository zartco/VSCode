# U1.5 Square Roots

## Learning Objectives
- Perform math operations on polynomials.
- Understand and calculate square roots to solve polynomial equations and geometric problems.

## Introduction
Understanding square roots is essential for solving a variety of algebraic and geometric problems, including polynomial equations.

Consider a square room that is $10 \text{ feet}$ by $10 \text{ feet}$. The formula for the area of a square is $A = s^2$, where $s$ is the side length.
To find the area in square feet, you multiply:
$10 \times 10 = 100 \text{ ft}^2$

Now consider the reverse question: If you are told that a square room needs $121 \text{ ft}^2$ of carpet, what are the dimensions of the room? This requires determining what number times itself equals $121$.
$11 \cdot 11 = 121$
$\sqrt{121} = 11$

## Definitions
- **Square Root:** The square root of a number is a value that, when multiplied by itself, gives the original number. For example, the square root of $121$ is $11$ because $11 \cdot 11 = 121$.
- **Radical Symbol:** The symbol $\sqrt{\phantom{x}}$ used to indicate a root.
- **Radicand:** The value inside of the square root symbol. In $\sqrt{121}$, the radicand is $121$.
- **Principal Square Root:** The positive square root of a number. Every positive number has two square roots: one positive and one negative (e.g., $11$ and $-11$ are both square roots of $121$, since $(-11) \cdot (-11) = 121$). The positive square root is referred to as the principal square root.

## Principal vs. Negative Square Roots
When should you use only the principal square root, and when should you use both? The context or the mathematical notation will generally provide clues.

### Guidelines for Finding Square Roots

| Problem / Context | Answer | Explanation |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| Use the Pythagorean theorem to determine the length of a hypotenuse. | **Principal square root only** | A length or dimension cannot be negative. |
| Find $\pm\sqrt{25}$. | **Positive and negative roots** ($\pm5$) | The $\pm$ symbol explicitly indicates both roots should be found. |
| What is $\sqrt{247}$? | **Principal square root only** | When only the radical $\sqrt{\phantom{x}}$ is given, it denotes the principal square root. (For both roots, the problem would ask for $\pm\sqrt{247}$). |
| Find all values of $x$: $x^2 = 36$. | **Positive and negative roots** ($x = 6, x = -6$) | The problem asks for all values of $x$ that satisfy the equation. |
| Find $-\sqrt{225}$. | **Negative square root only** ($-15$) | The negative sign outside the radical indicates only the negative root should be found. |

> [!NOTE] 
> In a context about the size of a room or the length of a square piece of paper, a negative length doesn't make sense, even though $(-11) \cdot (-11) = 121$ and $(-13) \cdot (-13) = 169$. That is why you use the principal square root for lengths and dimensions.

## Examples

**Example 1: Determine the principal square root of $64$.**
Find the positive number that results in a product of $64$ when multiplied by itself.
$8 \cdot 8 = 64$
$\sqrt{64} = 8$
The principal square root of $64$ is $8$.

**Example 2: Find the principal square root of $81$.**
$\sqrt{81} = 9$ (since $9 \cdot 9 = 81$)

**Example 3: Find the principal square root of $(-28)^2$.**
$\sqrt{(-28)^2} = \sqrt{784} = 28$
*(Alternatively, the principal square root of any squared real number is its absolute value: $\sqrt{x^2} = |x|$, so $\sqrt{(-28)^2} = |-28| = 28$.)*

**Example 4: A small office is $49 \text{ ft}^2$. If the office is square, how long is each side?**
Since side length must be positive, we find the principal square root of the area.
$\sqrt{49} = 7 \text{ feet}$.

## Beyond Square Roots

> [!INFO] Other Types of Roots
> The square root is not the only kind of root in mathematics. For example, the third, or **cube root**, of a number finds the value that, when multiplied by itself three times, equals the original number. 
> For example, the cube root of $8$ is $2$, because $2 \times 2 \times 2 = 2^3 = 8$.
